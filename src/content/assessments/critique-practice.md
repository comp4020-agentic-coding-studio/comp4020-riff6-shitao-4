## The brief

> Show that the crit format actually changed what you built, more than once,
> in both directions.

This course runs on the studio-then-crit shape from week 2 onward, and it's
easy to sit through eleven of them and let none of the feedback land. This
assessment asks for the opposite: evidence that a specific piece of feedback
in a specific studio changed a specific decision in your build, more than
once across the semester, and that you did the same for at least two other
people.

## What you submit

A short document (no fixed length, but three well-chosen pairs beat ten thin
ones): for each entry, name the studio, the feedback, and the change it
produced --- in your own build, or in someone else's, where you were the one
giving it.

The weighted criteria above put most of the mark on the thing this assessment
exists to judge --- whether feedback actually changed a decision --- and the
rest on what makes that claim checkable: how specific the evidence is, what
you gave others, how far across the semester it reaches, and whether the
account is honest about the times it didn't work.

The due date sits a few days after the final studio, not on it: this
portfolio is asked to cover every studio "this one included," and you can't
write down what you gave or got in a crit before you've sat through it.
