# Policies and support

<p class="lead">
  The course-wide rules for late work, extensions, declared assistance and
  getting help.
</p>

## Late work and extensions

No late submissions are accepted after a deadline passes, in either direction:
an instrument that only starts working the day after First Instrument was due
is marked as not submitted, not as submitted-with-a-penalty. Extensions exist
instead of a penalty scheme, and have to be arranged with [Marisol](/people/marisol-quaye/)
before the deadline, not after it --- a request made once a deadline has
already passed is asking for a different thing than an extension.

This is a stricter rule than most late-work schemes, and it's deliberate: an
instrument that has to run unattended for a stranger in week 12 is exactly the
kind of thing that fails quietly if "I'll finish it tonight" is allowed to
become a habit across the semester.

## Declared assistance

Say what you used to build each submission, and how: an autocomplete
suggestion accepted without reading it, an agent that wrote a whole file, a
conversation that only shaped how you thought about a constraint before you
wrote the code yourself. Put it in the submission repository's README, under
a heading of its own --- not scattered across commit messages a marker has to
reconstruct.

This isn't a disclosure rule borrowed from some other course: the whole
premise of studio time is a hand testing an instrument at the keyboard, not a
picture of one, so a build with an undeclared agent behind it is a different
claim than the one your crit was actually judging. None of these are
penalised on their own --- the [assessments page](/assessments/) and the
assessment briefs describe how each piece of work is actually marked. What's
marked down is a submission whose account of its own process doesn't match
what a marker can see in the repository once they go looking.

## Academic integrity

The work you submit is yours, with any assistance declared per the section
above. Presenting someone else's instrument, or the unattributed output of a
tool, as your own semester's constraint is misconduct handled under Slop
University's ordinary academic integrity process, not a course-specific one.

## Getting help

Ask during a studio first --- most of what a studio actually teaches is
diagnosing why a working build under your own hand behaves differently under
someone else's, and that's easiest to do live. [Idris](/people/idris-fenn/)
runs the office hours announced each studio for anything that can't wait.
Email either teacher for anything that shouldn't wait for the next studio at
all: a question about the brief, an access need, or a personal circumstance
affecting a deadline.
